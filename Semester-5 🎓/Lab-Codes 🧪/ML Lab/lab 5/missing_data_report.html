Handling Missing Data
Complete Case Analysis, Categorical and Numerical Imputation, KNN Imputer and Iterative Imputer

1. Objective

To explore, implement, and benchmark diverse methodologies for addressing missing data within an empirical dataset, and to assess their respective impacts on feature distributions, inter-variable relationships, and classification performance. The core objectives are:

  1. Detect, quantify, and visualize missing data patterns across tabular attributes.
  2. Execute Complete Case Analysis (listwise deletion) and analyze the consequences of observational loss.
  3. Apply univariate categorical and numerical imputation heuristics to rectify incomplete columns.
  4. Implement multivariate imputation models including K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) and Iterative Imputation (MICE).
  5. Perform a comparative evaluation of imputation strategies regarding distribution preservation and predictive utility.

2. Theory

Missing values occur frequently in real-world tabular data due to sensor dropouts, survey non-responses, or transmission interruptions. Inappropriate handling introduces substantial bias and destabilizes downstream predictive models. The five strategies examined in this experiment include:

Complete Case Analysis (Listwise Deletion): Discards every observation possessing one or more missing fields. While trivial to execute, it can induce severe sample reduction and catastrophic selection bias when data is not Missing Completely at Random (MCAR).

Categorical Imputation: Replaces missing qualitative levels with either the mode (most common category) or an explicit sentinel indicator such as "Unknown" to maintain structural category integrity.

Numerical Imputation: Substitutes numeric gaps with summary statistics (mean, median) or fixed constants (e.g., 0). While fast, univariate methods fail to capture feature covariance and compress variance.

KNN Imputer: Estimates missing feature entries by averaging values from the k nearest neighboring observations in Euclidean feature space, capturing localized multi-attribute patterns at the cost of distance computation overhead.

Iterative Imputer (MICE): Models each incomplete attribute as a dependent target regressed on all remaining features in round-robin fashion over repeated iterations, capturing complex multivariate linear combinations.

4. Dataset

The study utilizes the classic Titanic passenger dataset sourced directly from GitHub. It comprises 891 rows and 15 attributes, combining continuous/discrete numerical features (survived, pclass, age, sibsp, parch, fare) with categorical descriptors (sex, embarked, class, who, deck, embark_town, alive). The binary survival flag (survived) serves as the target variable for machine learning evaluation.

5. Procedure

  1. Import analytical libraries, ingest the Titanic dataset, and compute descriptive statistics.
  2. Quantify missing entries per attribute and visualize missingness density via a seaborn heatmap.
  3. Perform Complete Case Analysis using dropna() and compare pre- and post-deletion sample sizes.
  4. Impute missing qualitative features using mode substitution and the category "Unknown".
  5. Impute missing numerical ages using mean, median, and constant (0) values, contrasting resulting density curves.
  6. Apply the multivariate KNN Imputer (k=3) across numerical features.
  7. Apply the multivariate Iterative Imputer (max_iter=10) across numerical features.
  8. Benchmark initial classification accuracy by fitting Logistic Regression models across each imputed dataset.
  9. Calculate descriptive statistics for age across all techniques and visualize comparative density curves.
  10. Assess correlation matrices before and after imputation to evaluate preservation of inter-feature dependencies.
  11. Examine the iterative convergence trajectory and screen for out-of-domain (negative) imputed age values.
  12. Evaluate overall model test accuracy across all strategies, validating results using leakage-safe scikit-learn Pipelines.

Figure 2: Age distribution before and after mean, median and constant imputation

8. Observations

  1. Sample Size Preservation: Complete Case Analysis discards 79.6% of the dataset (collapsing sample size from 891 to 182 rows), whereas all imputation strategies preserve the entire 891 records. Univariate methods assign a single identical value to all 177 missing entries, while KNN and Iterative Imputation generate 59 and 94 distinct, personalized values, respectively.
  2. Distribution Integrity: Mean and median filling compress standard deviation from 14.53 down to ~13.00, introducing artificial mode spikes. Constant zero replacement creates an unphysical spike at 0, while KNN (std = 13.56) and Iterative Imputation (std = 13.70) retain natural distribution spread.
  3. Preservation of Inter-Feature Covariance: Univariate methods dilute correlations, constant filling distorts relationship signs, KNN preserves class associations, and Iterative regression accentuates linear feature dependencies.
  4. Rapid Convergence: The Iterative Imputer converged in just 2 iterations due to isolated numerical missingness in age.
  5. Predictive Utility: Multivariate imputers deliver superior classification accuracy (0.7598 uncontained; 0.7486 in leak-free pipelines), establishing clear performance advantages over data deletion and constant filling.

9. Result

Missing values in the Titanic dataset were localized to four features: age (177 missing, 19.9%), deck (688 missing, 77.2%), embarked (2 missing, 0.2%), and embark_town (2 missing, 0.2%). Complete Case Analysis discarded 709 records (79.6%), leaving a non-representative sub-sample dominated by first-class survivors (86% vs 24% baseline). Categorical gaps were effectively mitigated using mode substitution (port variables) and sentinel categorization ("Unknown" for cabin deck). For numerical age values, multivariate modeling via KNN and bounded Iterative Imputation maintained natural feature variance (std 13.56 and 13.70 vs. 14.53 raw) and avoided artificial mode spikes. Seven anomalous negative predictions in Iterative regression were resolved via min_value=0. Classification benchmarks confirmed the superiority of multivariate methods, achieving 0.7598 accuracy in global splits and 0.7486 in leak-free pipelines, outperforming median filling (0.7374), constant replacement (0.7151), and listwise deletion (0.7297).

10. Conclusion

Selecting an appropriate missing-data strategy fundamentally influences dataset dimensionality, feature distributions, and covariate dependencies. Listwise deletion is viable only under negligible, completely random missingness; when data loss is substantial, deletion induces severe selection bias. Univariate imputation provides a quick baseline but distorts variance and attenuates correlations. Multivariate approaches—specifically KNN and MICE Iterative Imputation—generate context-aware estimates that safeguard statistical distributions, though domain bounds (such as non-negative constraints) must be enforced. Categorical and numerical attributes require tailored treatments.

Finally, missing data imputation must be executed within cross-validation pipelines following train-test partitioning. Fitting imputers globally leaks test partition distributions and label associations into training workflows, inflating performance expectations. Encapsulating preprocessing inside scikit-learn Pipeline objects guarantees methodological integrity and authentic model generalization.

11. Discussion Questions

Q1. What are the drawbacks of complete case analysis?
  • Substantial Information Loss: Discarding all records with any missing attribute dramatically contracts sample volume. In our Titanic analysis, 79.6% of data was purged, primarily due to cabin deck gaps.
  • Severe Selection Bias: If missingness is not Missing Completely at Random (MCAR), surviving records misrepresent the population. Complete cases in the Titanic data exhibited an 86% first-class proportion and a 68% survival rate, compared to 24% and 38% in the raw dataset.
  • Impaired Model Stability: Training and evaluating on heavily diminished datasets (145 train, 37 test rows) produces unstable parameters and unreliable performance metrics.
  • Dimensional Vulnerability: As feature counts expand, the probability of an observation containing at least one unobserved attribute approaches certainty.
  • Inoperability during Deployment: A model requiring complete observations fails when deployed against real-world inputs containing missing values.
Q2. How does KNN Imputer differ from mean or median imputation?

Univariate mean or median imputation replaces every missing entry with a single global statistic, ignoring all companion features. In contrast, the KNN Imputer identifies the k nearest neighbors in multidimensional space and computes a localized, distance-weighted estimate. Key differences include:

  • Diverse Imputed Values: KNN produced 59 distinct imputed age values, whereas mean and median assign a single repeated number to all 177 missing entries.
  • Variance & Covariance Preservation: KNN preserved feature variance (std = 13.56 vs 13.00 for mean) and maintained class correlation (−0.385 vs −0.331).
  • Computational Overhead & Scale Sensitivity: KNN requires computing pairwise distances across all records, making it computationally intensive and sensitive to feature scaling (e.g., unbounded fare values).
Q3. In what scenarios is Iterative Imputer preferred?
  • When features exhibit strong linear or non-linear correlations, allowing unobserved values to be predicted from companion attributes.
  • When missingness spans multiple attributes simultaneously, enabling round-robin regression models to iteratively refine estimates.
  • When data satisfies the Missing at Random (MAR) assumption, where missingness depends on observed features.
  • When sample sizes are sufficiently large to support stable multivariate regression estimators without overfitting.

Application Precautions: Practitioners must enforce physical domain bounds (such as min_value=0 for age) to prevent unbounded linear extrapolation from producing negative estimates, and recognize that regression-based imputation tends to amplify existing collinearities.

Q4. Why is it important to treat categorical and numerical missing data differently?

Qualitative and quantitative features possess fundamentally distinct mathematical properties:

  • Mathematical Invalidity: Arithmetic operations (mean, variance, Euclidean distance) are meaningless on qualitative labels (e.g., deck letters or ports of embarkation). Categorical gaps must be resolved via mode substitution or explicit categorization.
  • Statistical Treatment: Numerical data can be addressed through parametric or non-parametric continuous estimators (mean, median, regression, KNN) selected based on distribution symmetry.
  • Informational Content of Missingness: For qualitative features with massive missingness (such as deck at 77%), mode imputation fabricates non-existent facts, whereas creating an "Unknown" level preserves non-response as a predictive indicator.
  • Algorithm Prerequisites: Distance-based and regression imputers require numerical inputs, necessitating dedicated qualitative encoding or separate handling.
Q5. What are some risks of over-imputation?
  • Fabrication of Synthetic Information: Imputing features dominated by missingness (such as 77% missing cabin decks) invents data never collected in reality.
  • Variance Compression & Deceptive Precision: Univariate imputation shrinks sample variance (depressing age std from 14.53 to 13.00), artificially inflating model confidence.
  • Distortion of Covariance Structures: Constant replacement introduced sign reversals in the age-survived relationship (+0.011 vs −0.077).
  • Generation of Out-of-Domain Values: Unbounded regression produced seven negative passenger ages (−5.07 years).
  • Data Contamination & Optimism Bias: Imputing across entire datasets prior to partitioning leaks test distributions and target labels, yielding deceptive validation performance (0.7598 vs. 0.7486 leak-free).
  • Suppression of Informative Missingness: In many operational domains, the absence of data is itself highly predictive; indiscriminately filling all gaps strips away critical diagnostic signals.
